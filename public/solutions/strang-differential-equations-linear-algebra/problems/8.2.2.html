Why is row iii of F‾\overline{F}F the same as row N−iN - iN−i of FFF (numbered from 000 to N−1N - 1N−1)?

I will switch the index to kkk as the book’s choice is confusing. One intuitive, geometric analogy to make here is F‾\overline{F}F reverses the direction of the roots of unity starting from zero. As we move down the Fourier matrix row by row, instead of moving counterclockwise around the unit circle to the kkk-th step, the conjugate changes the sign, causing us to move in the clockwise direction.

The other argument is the powers of ω=ei2π/N\omega = e^{i 2 \pi / N}ω=ei2π/N are computed modulo NNN. The kkk-th row of F‾\overline{F}F has ω=e−i2kπ/N\omega = e^{-i 2k \pi / N}ω=e−i2kπ/N and the N−kN - kN−k-th row of FFF has ω=ei2π ⁣(N−k)/N=e−i2kπ/N\omega = e^{i 2 \pi \!\left( N - k \right)} / N = e^{-i 2k \pi / N}ω=ei2π(N−k)/N=e−i2kπ/N. In this sense, row “NNN” of FFF simply corresponds to row 0: which is a row of 1’s in both of FFF and F‾\overline{F}F.
