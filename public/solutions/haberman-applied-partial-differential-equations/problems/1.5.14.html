Isobars are lines of constant temperature. Show that isobars are perpendicular to any part of the boundary that is insulated.

I believe the author means isotherms, which are level curves for temperature. Isobars are for pressure. By definition, the temperature distribution along an isotherm is constant, meaning u ⁣(r)=cu \!\left( r \right) = cu(r)=c. Additionally, from the construction of a gradient, we know that it is always perpendicular to level curves at any point. Along the normal vector n^\pmb{\hat{n}}n^, if we have an insulated boundary, then ∇u⋅n^=0\nabla u \cdot \pmb{\hat{n}} = 0∇u⋅n^=0. Since the gradient is orthogonal to both the isotherm and the normal vector, they must be parallel, demonstrating that isotherms must be orthogonal to any part of an insulated boundary.
