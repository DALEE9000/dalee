Prove that the temperature satisfying Laplace’s equation cannot attain its minimum in the interior.

If uuu is non-constant and a solution to Laplace’s equation, then so is −u-u−u. From the maximum principle, if −u-u−u cannot attain its maximum value in the interior, then uuu cannot attain its minimum value in the interior. Physically, suppose uuu was the minimum temperature in the interior at a point PPP. Then by diffusion, the temperature at point PPP would rise, and cease to be the minimum, contradicting the steady-stateness of PPP.
