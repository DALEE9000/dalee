Although the gradient, divergence, and curl theorems are the fundamental integral theorems of vector calculus, it is possible to derive a number of corollaries from them. Show that:

  1. ∫V ⁣(∇T) dτ=∮ST da\int_{\mathcal{V}} \!\left( \nabla T \right) \, d \tau = \oint_{\mathcal{S}} T \, d \pmb{a}∫V​(∇T)dτ=∮S​Tda. [Hint: Let v=cT\pmb{v} = \pmb{c}Tv=cT, where c\pmb{c}c is a constant, in the divergence theorem; use the product rules.]

  2. ∫V ⁣(∇×v) dτ=−∮Sv×da\int_{\mathcal{V}} \!\left( \nabla \times \pmb{v} \right) \, d \tau = -\oint_{\mathcal{S}} \pmb{v} \times d \pmb{a}∫V​(∇×v)dτ=−∮S​v×da. [Hint: Replace v\pmb{v}v by  ⁣(v×c)\!\left( \pmb{v} \times \pmb{c} \right)(v×c) in the divergence theorem.]

  3. ∫V ⁣[T∇2U+ ⁣(∇T)⋅ ⁣(∇U)] dτ=∮S ⁣(T∇U)⋅da\int_{\mathcal{V}} \!\left[ T \nabla^{2}U + \!\left( \nabla T \right) \cdot \!\left( \nabla U \right) \right] \, d \tau = \oint_{\mathcal{S}} \!\left( T \nabla U \right) \cdot d \pmb{a}∫V​[T∇2U+(∇T)⋅(∇U)]dτ=∮S​(T∇U)⋅da. [Hint: Let v=T∇U\pmb{v} = T \nabla Uv=T∇U in the divergence theorem.]

  4. ∫V ⁣(T∇2U−U∇2T) dτ=∮S ⁣(T∇U−U∇T)⋅da\int_{\mathcal{V}} \!\left( T \nabla^{2}U - U \nabla^{2}T \right) \, d \tau = \oint_{\mathcal{S}} \!\left( T \nabla U - U \nabla T \right) \cdot d \pmb{a}∫V​(T∇2U−U∇2T)dτ=∮S​(T∇U−U∇T)⋅da. [Comment: This is sometimes called Green’s second identity; it follows from (c), which is known as Green’s identity.]

  5. ∫S∇T×da=−∮PT dl\int_{\mathcal{S}} \nabla T \times d \pmb{a} = -\oint_{\mathcal{P}} T \, d \pmb{l}∫S​∇T×da=−∮P​Tdl. [Hint: Let v=cT\pmb{v} = \pmb{c}Tv=cT in Stokes’ theorem.]

  1. To clarify, c\pmb{c}c is a constant vector. Let v=cT\pmb{v} = \pmb{c}Tv=cT. Then ∇⋅v=c⋅ ⁣(∇T)+T ⁣(∇⋅c)=c⋅ ⁣(∇T)\nabla \cdot \pmb{v} = \pmb{c} \cdot \!\left( \nabla T \right) + T \!\left( \nabla \cdot \pmb{c} \right) = \pmb{c} \cdot \!\left( \nabla T \right)∇⋅v=c⋅(∇T)+T(∇⋅c)=c⋅(∇T) By the divergence theorem and pulling the constant vector out of the integral, c⋅∫V ⁣(∇T) dτ=c⋅∮ST da\pmb{c} \cdot \int_{\mathcal{V}} \!\left( \nabla T \right) \, d \tau = \pmb{c} \cdot \oint_{\mathcal{S}} T \, d \pmb{a}c⋅∫V​(∇T)dτ=c⋅∮S​Tda Since c\pmb{c}c can be any arbitrary constant vector, this forces equality of the (vector) integrals.

  2. Compute ∇⋅ ⁣(v×c)=c⋅ ⁣(∇×v)−v⋅ ⁣(∇×c)=c⋅ ⁣(∇×v)\nabla \cdot \!\left( \pmb{v} \times \pmb{c} \right) = \pmb{c} \cdot \!\left( \nabla \times \pmb{v} \right) - \pmb{v} \cdot \!\left( \nabla \times \pmb{c} \right) = \pmb{c} \cdot \!\left( \nabla \times \pmb{v} \right)∇⋅(v×c)=c⋅(∇×v)−v⋅(∇×c)=c⋅(∇×v) By the divergence theorem, c⋅∫V ⁣(∇×v) dτ=∮S ⁣(v×c)⋅da\pmb{c} \cdot \int_{\mathcal{V}} \!\left( \nabla \times \pmb{v} \right) \, d \tau = \oint_{\mathcal{S}} \!\left( \pmb{v} \times \pmb{c} \right) \cdot d \pmb{a}c⋅∫V​(∇×v)dτ=∮S​(v×c)⋅da Rewrite the integrand of the right term as − ⁣(c×v)⋅da-\!\left( \pmb{c} \times \pmb{v} \right) \cdot d \pmb{a}−(c×v)⋅da Since dot products commute, this is equal to −da⋅ ⁣(c×v)=−c⋅ ⁣(v×da)-d \pmb{a} \cdot \!\left( \pmb{c} \times \pmb{v} \right) = -\pmb{c} \cdot \!\left( \pmb{v} \times d \pmb{a} \right)−da⋅(c×v)=−c⋅(v×da) Therefore our divergence theorem undergoes a makeover to get c⋅∫V ⁣(∇×v) dτ=−c⋅∮Sv×da\pmb{c} \cdot \int_{\mathcal{V}} \!\left( \nabla \times \pmb{v} \right) \, d \tau = -\pmb{c} \cdot \oint_{\mathcal{S}} \pmb{v} \times d \pmb{a}c⋅∫V​(∇×v)dτ=−c⋅∮S​v×da And from the argument of arbitrary c\pmb{c}c in the previous derivation, we derive the desired result.

  3. Let v=T∇U\pmb{v} = T \nabla Uv=T∇U. Then ∇⋅T∇U=T∇2U+ ⁣(∇T)⋅ ⁣(∇U)\nabla \cdot T \nabla U = T \nabla^{2}U + \!\left( \nabla T \right) \cdot \!\left( \nabla U \right)∇⋅T∇U=T∇2U+(∇T)⋅(∇U) By the divergence theorem, ∫V ⁣[T∇2U+ ⁣(∇T)⋅ ⁣(∇U)] dτ=∮S ⁣(T∇U)⋅da\int_{\mathcal{V}} \!\left[ T \nabla^{2}U + \!\left( \nabla T \right) \cdot \!\left( \nabla U \right) \right] \, d \tau = \oint_{\mathcal{S}} \!\left( T \nabla U \right) \cdot d \pmb{a}∫V​[T∇2U+(∇T)⋅(∇U)]dτ=∮S​(T∇U)⋅da

  4. Subtract ∫V ⁣[U∇2T+ ⁣(∇T)⋅ ⁣(∇U)] dτ=∮S ⁣(U∇T)⋅da\int_{\mathcal{V}} \!\left[ U \nabla^{2}T + \!\left( \nabla T \right) \cdot \!\left( \nabla U \right) \right] \, d \tau = \oint_{\mathcal{S}} \!\left( U \nabla T \right) \cdot d \pmb{a}∫V​[U∇2T+(∇T)⋅(∇U)]dτ=∮S​(U∇T)⋅da from the result in (c).

  5. Let v=cT\pmb{v} = \pmb{c}Tv=cT. Its curl is ∇×cT=T ⁣(∇×c)−c× ⁣(∇T)=−c× ⁣(∇T)\nabla \times \pmb{c}T = T \!\left( \nabla \times \pmb{c} \right) - \pmb{c} \times \!\left( \nabla T \right) = -\pmb{c} \times \!\left( \nabla T \right)∇×cT=T(∇×c)−c×(∇T)=−c×(∇T) which, when dotted with dad \pmb{a}da, produces da⋅ ⁣[−c× ⁣(∇T)]=−c⋅ ⁣(∇T×da)d \pmb{a} \cdot \!\left[ -\pmb{c} \times \!\left( \nabla T \right) \right] = - \pmb{c} \cdot \!\left( \nabla T \times d \pmb{a} \right)da⋅[−c×(∇T)]=−c⋅(∇T×da) By Stokes’ theorem, we get c⋅∫S∇T×da=−c⋅∮PT dl\pmb{c} \cdot \int_{\mathcal{S}} \nabla T \times d \pmb{a} = -\pmb{c} \cdot \oint_{\mathcal{P}} T \, d \pmb{l}c⋅∫S​∇T×da=−c⋅∮P​Tdl Once again, by the arbitrariness of c\pmb{c}c, it must be true that the integrals are equal.
