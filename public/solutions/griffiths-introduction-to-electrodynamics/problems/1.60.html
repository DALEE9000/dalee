Here are two cute checks of the fundamental theorems:

  1. Combine Corollary 2 to the gradient theorem with Stokes’ theorem (v=∇T\pmb{v} = \nabla Tv=∇T, in this case). Show that the result is consistent with what you already knew about second derivatives.

  2. Combine Corollary 2 to Stokes’ theorem with the divergence theorem. Show that the result is consistent with what you already knew.

  1. Corollary 2 states that ∮ ⁣(∇T)⋅dl=0\oint \!\left( \nabla T \right) \cdot d \pmb{l} = 0∮(∇T)⋅dl=0. By Stokes’ theorem, ∫ ⁣(∇×∇T)⋅da=0\int \!\left( \nabla \times \nabla T \right) \cdot d \pmb{a} = 0∫(∇×∇T)⋅da=0, which is consistent with the fact that the curl of a gradient is always zero.

  2. Corollary 2 states that ∮ ⁣(∇×v)⋅da=0\oint \!\left( \nabla \times \pmb{v} \right) \cdot d \pmb{a} = 0∮(∇×v)⋅da=0. By the divergence theorem, ∫ ⁣(∇⋅ ⁣(∇×v)) dτ=0\int \!\left( \nabla \cdot \!\left( \nabla \times \pmb{v} \right) \right) \, d \tau = 0∫(∇⋅(∇×v))dτ=0, which is consistent with the fact that the divergence of a curl is always zero.

Suppose we had not known these facts. In both cases, it is the arbitrariness of the surface and volume, respectively, that forces the integrand to zero, and establishes the second derivative vanishing identities.
