(This problem is taken from Probability and Statistical Inference for Engineers by Derman and Klein, Oxford University Press, London, 1959.) The number of oil tankers, say N\bm{N}N, arriving at a certain refinery each day has a Poisson distribution with parameter λ=2\bm{\lambda = 2}λ=2. Present port facilities can service three tankers a day. If more than three tankers arrive in a day, the tankers in excess of three must be sent to another port.

For each problem below,

P(X=N)=e−2⋅2NN!P(X = N) = \frac{e^{-2} \cdot 2^N}{N!}P(X=N)=N!e−2⋅2N​

  1. On a given day, what is the probability of having to send tankers away?

    We calculate

    P(X>3)=1−(P(X=3)+P(X=2)+P(X=1)+P(X=0))=1−(8e−26+4e−22+2e−2+e−2)≈0.1429\begin{align*} P(X > 3) &= 1 - (P(X = 3) + P(X = 2) + P(X = 1) + P(X = 0)) \\ &= 1 - \Big( \frac{8e^{-2}}{6} + \frac{4 e^{-2}}{2} + 2e^{-2} + e^{-2} \Big) \\ &\approx \boxed{0.1429} \end{align*}P(X>3)​=1−(P(X=3)+P(X=2)+P(X=1)+P(X=0))=1−(68e−2​+24e−2​+2e−2+e−2)≈0.1429​​

  2. How much must present facilities be increased to permit handling all tankers on approximately 90 percent of the days?

    We want to find the smallest kkk such that P(X>k)≥0.9P(X > k) \geq 0.9P(X>k)≥0.9. It turns out this k=4\boxed{k = 4}k=4​, as P(X>4)=1−∑k=04e−2⋅2kk!≈0.0527P(X > 4) = 1 - \sum^4_{k = 0} \frac{e^{-2} \cdot 2^k }{k!} \approx 0.0527P(X>4)=1−∑k=04​k!e−2⋅2k​≈0.0527.

  3. What is the expected number of tankers arriving per day?

    By Meyer Theorem 8.1, the expectation of a Poisson distribution is its parameter, so the expectation for the number of tankers NNN is E[N]=2\boxed{E[N] = 2}E[N]=2​.

  4. What is the most probable number of tankers arriving daily?

    From Problem 8.2, for a natural number parameter, the maxima probabilities are at X=λX = \lambdaX=λ and X=λ−1X = \lambda - 1X=λ−1. Therefore the most probable numbers of tankers arriving daily are N=1,2\boxed{N = 1, 2}N=1,2​.

  5. What is the expected number of tankers serviced daily?

    Each of the outcomes that 0, 1, or 2 tankers are serviced has a unique Poisson probability associated with it, namely the probability of that number of tankers arriving that day. However, the event that 3 tankers are serviced is not only associated with the probability that 3 tankers arrive, but also that 4, 5, ... and so-forth arrive. Let X1X_1X1​ be the number of tankers serviced. Then

    E[X1]=∑k=02k⋅e−2⋅2kk!+3∑k=3∞e−2⋅2kk!≈1.782E[X_1] = \sum^2_{k = 0} k \cdot \frac{e^{-2} \cdot 2^k}{k!} + 3 \sum^{\infty}_{k = 3} \frac{e^{-2} \cdot 2^k}{k!} \approx \boxed{1.782}E[X1​]=k=0∑2​k⋅k!e−2⋅2k​+3k=3∑∞​k!e−2⋅2k​≈1.782​

  6. What is the expected number of tankers turned away daily?

    Here, the intuition is that the probability of 0 tankers being turned away is equal to the sum of the probabilities that 0, 1, 2, or 3 tankers arrive. Proceeding, the probability of 1 tanker turned away is that of 4 tankers arriving, 2 turned away that of 5 arriving, and so on. Let X2X_2X2​ be the number of tankers turned away. Thus we can express the expectation of the number of turned away tankers as

    E[X2]=∑k=1∞k⋅e−2⋅2k+3(k+3)!≈0.218E[X_2] = \sum^{\infty}_{k = 1} k \cdot \frac{e^{-2} \cdot 2^{k+3}}{(k+3)!} \approx \boxed{0.218}E[X2​]=k=1∑∞​k⋅(k+3)!e−2⋅2k+3​≈0.218​
