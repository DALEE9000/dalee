Twenty items, 12 of which are defective and 8 nondefective, are inspected one after the other. If these items are chosen at random, what is the probability that:

  1. the first two items inspected are defective?

    The number of arrangements with the first two items fixed as defective over the total number of arrangements of defective and nondefective items, or (1810)/(2012)=33/95\binom{18}{10} / \binom{20}{12} = \boxed{33/95}(1018​)/(1220​)=33/95​.   2. the first two items inspected are nondefective?

    (186)/(2012)=14/95\binom{18}{6} / \binom{20}{12} = \boxed{14/95}(618​)/(1220​)=14/95​, or (7/19)⋅(8/20)=14/95(7/19) \cdot (8/20) = \boxed{14/95}(7/19)⋅(8/20)=14/95​.

  3. among the first two items inspected there is one defective and one nondefective?

    Method 1. Fixing one of the first two positions as nondefective, he number of combinations to place the remaining 7 nondefective items in the remaining 18 positions is (187)\binom{18}{7}(718​). Analogously, the number of combinations to place the remaining 11 defective items in the remaining 18 positions, fixing one in one of the first two positions, is (1811)\binom{18}{11}(1118​). Notice that the 18 in the top of the binomial coefficient is how we properly tabulate that the first two of the twenty positions are fixed, i.e. only 18 positions remain free to be occupied. Summing the combinations and dividing by (2012)\binom{20}{12}(1220​) yields ((187)+(1811))/(2012)=48/95( \binom{18}{7} + \binom{18}{11} ) / \binom{20}{12} = \boxed{48/95}((718​)+(1118​))/(1220​)=48/95​. The intuition behind summing the combinations is because the positions of the defective and nondefective items in the first two positions can be flipped – we do not specify that one or the other necessarily has to be in the first or second position, only that one of each are among the first two positions.

    Method 2. By the law of total probability, (8/20)(12/19)+(12/20)(8/19)=48/95(8/20) (12/19) + (12/20) (8/19) = \boxed{48/95}(8/20)(12/19)+(12/20)(8/19)=48/95​.
