A lot contains n\bm{n}n articles. If it is known that r\bm{r}r of the articles are defective and the articles are inspected in a random order, what is the probability that the k\bm{k}k-th article (k≥r)\bm{(k \geq r)}(k≥r) inspected will be the last defective one in the lot?

To motivate this problem, first imagine every possible combination of the nnn articles arranged in a line. Recognize that there are (nr)\binom{n}{r}(rn​) total positions that the rrr articles can take in the line of nnn articles; this is equivalent to choosing rrr objects from nnn. The trickier insight is to ascertain how many arrangements of the rrr defective articles there are if the last of the rrr articles is in the kkk-th position. To do so, fix the rrr-th article in the kkk-th position. Then there are r−1r-1r−1 remaining objects to arrange in the k−1k-1k−1 remaining positions. Simply, there are (k−1r−1)\binom{k-1}{r-1}(r−1k−1​) ways to do this. Therefore, the probability that the kkk-th article (k≥r)(k \geq r)(k≥r) inspected will be the last defective one in the lot is (k−1r−1)/(nr)\boxed{\binom{k-1}{r-1} / \binom{n}{r}}(r−1k−1​)/(rn​)​.
