A machinist keeps a large number of washers in a drawer. About 50 percent of these washers are 14\bm{\frac{1}{4}}41​ inch in diameter, about 30 percent are 18\bm{\frac{1}{8}}81​ inch in diameter, and the remaining 20 percent are 38\bm{\frac{3}{8}}83​ inch in diameter. Suppose that 10 washers are chosen at random.

  1. What is the probability that there are exactly five 14\bm{\frac{1}{4}}41​-inch washers, four 18\bm{\frac{1}{8}}81​-inch washers, and one 38\bm{\frac{3}{8}}83​-inch washer?

    Let X1=X_1 =X1​= the number of size 1/41/41/4, X2=X_2 =X2​= the number of size 1/81/81/8, and X3=X_3 =X3​= the number of size 3/83/83/8, where P(X1)=0.5,P(X2)=0.3,P(X3)=0.2P(X_1) = 0.5, P(X_2) = 0.3, P(X_3) = 0.2P(X1​)=0.5,P(X2​)=0.3,P(X3​)=0.2, respectively. By the multinomial distribution,

    P(X1=5,X2=4,X3=1)=10!5!4!1!P(X1)5P(X2)4P(X3)≈0.0638P(X_1 = 5, X_2 = 4, X_3 = 1) = \frac{10!}{5! 4! 1!} P(X_1)^5 P(X_2)^4 P(X_3) \approx \boxed{0.0638}P(X1​=5,X2​=4,X3​=1)=5!4!1!10!​P(X1​)5P(X2​)4P(X3​)≈0.0638​

  2. What is the probability that only two kinds of washers are among the chosen ones?

    Here, we must consider all of the instances where we have only the 1/41/41/4 and 1/81/81/8, 1/41/41/4 and 3/83/83/8, and 1/81/81/8 and 3/83/83/8 washers. Moreover, for each pair, we consider each of the (10,10−i)(10, 10-i)(10,10−i) amount of each in the pair for i=0,...,10i = 0,..., 10i=0,...,10.     P(only two kinds of washers)=∑i=1910!(10−i)!i!P(X1)10−iP(X2)i+∑i=1910!(10−i)!i!P(X1)10−iP(X3)i+∑i=1910!(10−i)!i!P(X2)10−iP(X3)i≈0.135\begin{align*} P(\text{only two kinds of washers}) &= \sum^9_{i=1} \frac{10!}{(10-i)! i!} P(X_1)^{10-i} P(X_2)^i + \sum^9_{i=1} \frac{10!}{(10-i)! i!} P(X_1)^{10-i} P(X_3)^i \\ &\quad + \sum^9_{i=1} \frac{10!}{(10-i)! i!} P(X_2)^{10-i} P(X_3)^i \approx \boxed{0.135} \end{align*}P(only two kinds of washers)​=i=1∑9​(10−i)!i!10!​P(X1​)10−iP(X2​)i+i=1∑9​(10−i)!i!10!​P(X1​)10−iP(X3​)i+i=1∑9​(10−i)!i!10!​P(X2​)10−iP(X3​)i≈0.135​​

  3. What is the probability that all three kinds of washers are among the chosen ones?

    An equivalent question we can ask ourselves is, in what way can we express a sum of a triplet of numbers such that they all add to 10? Simply, how many ways can we write 1+1+8,1+2+7,2+2+61+1+8, 1+2+7, 2+2+61+1+8,1+2+7,2+2+6, and so-on-and-so-forth, not accounting for the order of numbers in the sum?

    One general expression for this sum can be

    i+j+(10−i−j)i + j + (10 - i - j)i+j+(10−i−j)

    And one insight we can gather here is, once iii and jjj have been determined, so has 10−i−j10 - i - j10−i−j. Now, we have an additional constraint that we must have three terms that are non-zero, so we also cannot have any of i,ji, ji,j, and 10−i−j10 - i - j10−i−j equaling 9. Effectively, this means that we can only iii or jjj run from 1,...,81, ..., 81,...,8. Say j=1,...,8j = 1,...,8j=1,...,8 without loss of generality. How do we now relate iii and jjj? Since iii must also run from 1,...,81, ..., 81,...,8, we may equivalently express this as iii can only run from 1,...,9−j1,..., 9-j1,...,9−j. We can now systematically see that when j=1j = 1j=1, 9−j=89-j = 89−j=8, and thus we may have i=1,...,8i = 1,..., 8i=1,...,8, and 10−i−j=8,...,110-i-j = 8, ..., 110−i−j=8,...,1, and so-on-and-so-forth when we run through j=2,...j = 2, ...j=2,... and when we reach j=8j = 8j=8, we must have i=1i = 1i=1, and 10−i−j=110 - i - j = 110−i−j=1. Expressing these insights in double summation form and in the desired probability, we can finally write

    P(all 3 kinds among chosen)=∑j=18∑i=19−j10!(10−i−j)!i!j!P(X1)jP(X2)iP(X3)10−i−j≈0.864P(\text{all 3 kinds among chosen}) = \sum^8_{j=1} \sum^{9-j}_{i=1} \frac{10!}{(10-i-j)! i! j!} P(X_1)^j P(X_2)^i P(X_3)^{10-i-j} \approx \boxed{ 0.864}P(all 3 kinds among chosen)=j=1∑8​i=1∑9−j​(10−i−j)!i!j!10!​P(X1​)jP(X2​)iP(X3​)10−i−j≈0.864​

    To further solidify the thoughts undergirding the above expression, I encourage the reader to write out each term of the double summation to see that, indeed, every possible triple sum of integers 1,...,81,...,81,...,8 adding to 10 is covered.

  4. What is the probability that there are three of one kind, three of another kind, and four of the third kind in a sample of 10?

    We can have either 4 of the first, second, or third kind of washer and 3 for the remaining two. Thus we have
