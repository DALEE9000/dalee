If from the lot of articles described in Problem 2.6 two articles are chosen (without replacement), find the probability that:

  1. both are good,

    Choose one of ten good articles out of sixteen total articles in the first round, followed by one of nine good articles out of the remaining fifteen articles in the second, invoke dependence of probabilities, 1016⋅915=3/8\frac{10}{16} \cdot \frac{9}{15} = \boxed{3/8}1610​⋅159​=3/8​.

    All sub-parts below proceed using similar logic of the product of probabilities.

  2. both have major defects,

    216⋅115=1/120\frac{2}{16} \cdot \frac{1}{15} = \boxed{1/120}162​⋅151​=1/120​

  3. at least one is good,

    Equivalently, unity less the probability that both are defective.

    1−616⋅515=1−30240=7/81 - \frac{6}{16} \cdot \frac{5}{15} = 1 - \frac{30}{240} = \boxed{7/8}1−166​⋅155​=1−24030​=7/8​

  4. at most one is good,

    Equivalently, unity less the probability that both are good.

    1−1016⋅915=1−3/8=581 - \frac{10}{16} \cdot \frac{9}{15} = 1 - 3/8 = \boxed{ \frac{5}{8} }1−1610​⋅159​=1−3/8=85​​

  5. exactly one is good,

    (101)(61)(162)=1/2\frac{\binom{10}{1} \binom{6}{1} }{\binom{16}{2}} = \boxed{1/2}(216​)(110​)(16​)​=1/2​

  6. neither has major defects,

    1416⋅1315=91/120\frac{14}{16} \cdot \frac{13}{15} = \boxed{91/120}1614​⋅1513​=91/120​

  7. neither is good.

    616⋅515=1/8\frac{6}{16} \cdot \frac{5}{15} = \boxed{1/8}166​⋅155​=1/8​
