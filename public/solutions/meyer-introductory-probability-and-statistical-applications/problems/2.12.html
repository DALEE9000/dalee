A mechanism may be set at any one of four positions, say a,b,c,\bm{a,b,c,}a,b,c, and d\bm{d}d. There are 8 such mechanisms which are inserted into a system.

  1. In how many ways may this system be set?

    Eight mechanisms which each can have one of four positions, so 48\boxed{4^8}48​ ways.

  2. Assume that these mechanisms are installed in some preassigned (linear) order. How many ways of setting the system are available if no two adjacent mechanisms are in the same position?

    The first of the mechanisms can take on any of the four positions. The adjacent mechanism, under the constraint, can only be placed into one of three positions. And so on and so forth for the remaining mechanisms. Therefore, 4⋅37\boxed{4 \cdot 3^7}4⋅37​ ways.

  3. How many ways are available if only positions a\bm{a}a and b\bm{b}b are used, and these are used equally often?

    A simpler way to think about this problem is to ask: for each of the eight mechanisms, how many ways can we choose four of them to place in position aaa? But this is merely (84)=70\binom{8}{4} = \boxed{70}(48​)=70​ ways.

  4. How many ways are available if only two different positions are used and one of these positions appears three times as often as the other?

    For any one pair, there are (86)=28\binom{8}{6} = 28(68​)=28 ways to arrange the mechanisms under the given constraint. There are (42)=6\binom{4}{2} = 6(24​)=6 different pairings. For each of those pairings, one of the positions will appear three times as often as the other, so the number of ways must be doubled to account for the other of the positions appearing three times as often as the initial. Therefore, there are 28⋅6⋅2=33628 \cdot 6 \cdot 2 = \boxed{336}28⋅6⋅2=336​ ways.
