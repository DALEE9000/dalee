Suppose that a satellite telemetering device receives two kinds of signals which may be recorded as real numbers, say X\bm{X}X and Y\bm{Y}Y. Assume that X\bm{X}X and Y\bm{Y}Y are independent, continuous random variables with pdf’s f\bm{f}f and g\bm{g}g, respectively. Suppose that during any specified period of time only one of these signals may be received and hence transmitted back to earth, namely that signal which arrives first. Assume furthermore that the signal giving rise to the value of X\bm{X}X arrives first with probability p\bm{p}p and hence the signal giving rise to Y\bm{Y}Y arrives first with probability 1−p\bm{1-p}1−p. Let Z\bm{Z}Z denote the random variable whose value is actually received and transmitted.

  1. Express the pdf of Z\bm{Z}Z in terms of f\bm{f}f and g\bm{g}g.

    (1−p)∫abg(y) dy(1-p) \int^b_a g(y) \ dy(1−p)∫ab​g(y) dy

    h(z)=pf(x)+(1−p)g(y)\boxed{ h(z) = p f(x) + (1-p) g(y) }h(z)=pf(x)+(1−p)g(y)​

    which is a mixture of two normal distributions. The Kolmogorov axioms can be easily verified. By premise, we must have f(x),g(y)≥0f(x), g(y) \geq 0f(x),g(y)≥0, and p,1−p≥0p, 1-p \geq 0p,1−p≥0, so we must have h(z)≥0h(z) \geq 0h(z)≥0. Lastly,

    Since ZZZ takes on all of the values XXX and YYY do, namely the reals, we can write

    E[Z]=∫−∞+∞zh(z) dz=p∫−∞+∞xf(x) dx+(1−p)∫−∞+∞yg(y) dy=pE[X]+(1−p)E[Y]\begin{align*} E[Z] &= \int^{+\infty}_{-\infty} z h(z) \ dz = p \int^{+\infty}_{-\infty} x f(x) \ dx + (1-p) \int^{+\infty}_{-\infty} y g(y) \ dy \\ &= \boxed{p E[X] + (1-p) E[Y]} \end{align*}E[Z]​=∫−∞+∞​zh(z) dz=p∫−∞+∞​xf(x) dx+(1−p)∫−∞+∞​yg(y) dy=pE[X]+(1−p)E[Y]​​

    Intuitively, the expectation of ZZZ is a weighted average of the expectations of XXX and YYY.
