Ten chips numbered 1 through 10 are mixed in a bowl. Two chips numbered (X,Y)\bm{(X,Y)}(X,Y) are drawn from the bowl, successively and without replacement. What is the probability that X+Y=10\bm{X + Y = 10}X+Y=10?

Note: The principle to recall here is that of multiplicative probabilities: when two events A,B\bm{A, B}A,B are independent, the probability of both happening (P(A∩B)\bm{P(A \cap B)}P(A∩B)) is the product of the probabilities of the individual events, namely P(A)P(B)\bm{P(A)P(B)}P(A)P(B). Should the events be dependent, say B\bm{B}B depends on A\bm{A}A, then P(A∩B)=P(A)P(B∣A)\bm{P(A \cap B) = P(A) P(B | A)}P(A∩B)=P(A)P(B∣A) .

In the first drawing, there are 10 chips to choose from. In the second, there are only 9. By the multiplication principle, there are 90 possible outcomes. For the first round, drawing any chip suffices so long as that chip is not 5 or 10. We cannot choose 10, because no other chip summed with 10 will yield 10, and we also cannot choose 5, because we do not replace the chips, and there is no other number to which 5 can be summed to give us 10. Therefore, there is a 8/108/108/10 odds that chips 5 or 10 are not drawn. In the second round, only 9 chips remain, for which only one will correspond to the first chip to give 10, or a 1/9 chance of drawing that chip. By the multiplicative property of conditional probabilities of dependent events, (8/10)(1/9)=4/45(8/10)(1/9) = \boxed{4/45}(8/10)(1/9)=4/45​.
